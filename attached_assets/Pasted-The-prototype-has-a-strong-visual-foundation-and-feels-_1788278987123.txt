The prototype has a strong visual foundation and feels recognizably Memento. The homepage hero is especially successful. However, it is not ready to present as a finished website yet because several visible and functional issues would distract the client.

I tested the live prototype on desktop and a 400px mobile viewport, including the Experiences page, navigation, Gallery, Story, Contact and the first two booking steps. I did not submit a booking.

## What’s working

- The **Instrument Serif, Manrope and handwritten accent** create the editorial, nostalgic and quietly luxurious feeling the client requested.
- Warm ivory, ink and restrained faded rose feel sophisticated rather than wedding-only or “Canva-like.”
- The homepage hero is compelling. “Keep the moment” and “Printed. Shared. Remembered.” communicate the proposition quickly.
- Fine rules, negative space and the subtle grid successfully reference editorial posters and considered packaging.
- The Experiences page correctly explains that layouts and print formats can adapt per event—there is no misleading promise of one fixed branded strip.
- The primary package includes the agreed essentials.
- “Memento Experience” and “For Brands & Gatherings” are sensible customer-facing categories.
- Calls to action consistently lead toward requesting a date.
- The five-step booking structure is much less intimidating than one long form.
- The booking language correctly says it is an availability request rather than an automatic confirmation.
- WhatsApp and telephone numbers are correct and consistent.
- The header, footer and overall visual language remain cohesive across pages.

## Fix before the next client presentation

1. **Repair the mobile menu**

   On mobile, the navigation opens directly over “Make it physical.” The links collide with the headline and there is no proper menu panel.

   It needs a full-screen or solid ivory overlay, enough spacing between links, a visible close button and a prominent “Request a Date” action. Background scrolling should be locked while it is open.

2. **Reset the scroll position when changing pages**

   When I opened Gallery and The Story after scrolling another page, each new page retained the previous scroll position. The Story opened near its footer, and Gallery initially appeared completely blank.

   Every route change should automatically return the visitor to the top.

3. **Fix Gallery rendering**

   The Gallery heading appears, but much of the page remains visibly empty even while scrolling. The accessibility structure says images exist, so the reveal/lazy-loading animation may be leaving them invisible.

   This is a launch blocker because Gallery is essential evidence for a photography business.

4. **Replace the remaining placeholders**

   Visible gaps currently appear on:

   - Homepage portrait/archive sections
   - Both Experiences package sections
   - The Gallery
   - The Story

   The empty areas make the editorial spacing feel accidental rather than luxurious. Use selected candid photographs, physical prints, a photo strip and one tactile keepsake detail.

5. **Confirm the public pricing**

   The booking form currently displays:

   - RWF 150,000 per hour
   - Two-hour minimum
   - 30% deposit after approval

   These are significant commercial decisions. They should only remain if Winnie explicitly approved them. Otherwise, show “Pricing confirmed after reviewing your request,” because the rest of the website promises a tailored quote.

## Content that needs approval

- The add-ons currently read **Guestbook Archive, Custom Backdrops, Extended Hours and Bespoke Folios**. The agreed list was:

  - Keepsakes & Memory Books
  - Custom Backdrops
  - Branded Experiences

  Guestbook Archive is a beautiful interpretation of Memory Books, but Branded Experiences is missing. Extended Hours and Bespoke Folios shouldn’t be advertised unless the client can actually provide them.

- The Story page presents an invented founding narrative: “Memento was born…” and criticizes other booths for “cheap props” and “harsh ring lights.” That sounds unnecessarily negative and may not be the client’s real story. Replace it with a short, client-approved origin and focus on Memento’s beliefs.

- Claims that prints are “waterproof” and “guaranteed not to fade for decades” require evidence from the exact printer and paper supplier. Qualify or remove them.

- “Available for travel nationwide” also needs the client’s approval.

- The Gallery’s Instagram button currently links to generic `instagram.com`, not Memento’s account.

- Add the final Memento email address to Contact and the footer once Winnie creates it.

## Production and booking work still needed

- Enforce unavailable dates and booking conflicts on the server—not only visually.
- Prevent bookings before the official opening date if that restriction still applies.
- Notify Memento and the customer after a request.
- Generate and display the booking reference.
- Replace Clerk development credentials with a client-owned production account.
- Add spam/rate protection and test the complete submission.
- Add the final domain, favicon and social-sharing preview.
- Keep rose for accents; some smaller rose and grey text currently needs stronger contrast.

My overall assessment: **the creative direction is around 80% there, but the functional/public-ready website is closer to 65–70%**. The best next sequence is mobile navigation → route scrolling → Gallery rendering → approved imagery → copy/package corrections → booking logic and notifications.
